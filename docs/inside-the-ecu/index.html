Customer insight

Inside the ECU

What a remap actually changes, how we get into your engine's computer, and the kit we carry in the van. No jargon you don't need.

Three ways in

How we read your ECU

Most vehicles are done through the diagnostic port in the footwell. Newer or locked ECUs need more work, and that's where the bench and the soldering iron come in.

Through the OBD port

A cable into the diagnostic socket under the dash. Nothing comes apart. This covers most cars and vans and takes under an hour to read and write.

On the bench

The ECU is unplugged and connected to our tools through its own connector. Used when the port is locked or the manufacturer has blocked OBD writing.

Boot mode and BDM

The case is opened and we connect straight to the processor with probes or soldered wires. It's the deepest level of access and how bricked or damaged units are recovered.

What a remap edits

Inside a calibration

An engine ECU holds thousands of tables. These are the ones a remap changes. Each is a grid like the boost map on our home page.

Driver's wish

Converts pedal position into a torque request. Changing it sharpens throttle response.

Torque limiters

The factory ceilings on torque, by gear and engine speed. Often the biggest restriction on a diesel.

Boost target

How much pressure the turbo is asked to make at each rpm and load.

Smoke limiter

Caps fuel against measured air so the engine never runs rich enough to smoke.

Rail pressure

The fuel pressure at the injectors, which affects how finely the fuel is atomised.

Injection timing

When fuel is injected. Small changes here affect efficiency, noise and exhaust temperature.

Step by step

What happens on the day

  1. Book and check

    Send your reg. We confirm the ECU type, what's possible and the price before we set off.

  2. Health check on arrival

    Battery support connected, all modules scanned, live data checked for boost, rail pressure and injector corrections.

  3. Read and back up

    We read your original file through the OBD port, or on the bench if needed, and save it for life.

  4. Calibrate

    Boost, fuelling, timing, torque limiters and smoke maps adjusted for your engine and what you use the vehicle for.

  5. Write and verify

    The new file is written back, checksums are corrected and the ECU is verified before anything else.

  6. Road test and log

    We drive it with you, log the key values and hand back the keys. Most jobs take one to two hours.

Questions

Frequently asked

Will a remap damage my engine?

A good remap stays inside the limits of the standard turbo, injectors and clutch, and we check the vehicle is healthy before we start. Problems usually come from tuning a vehicle that already has a fault, or from generic files pushed too far. We do neither.

Do I need to tell my insurer?

Yes. A remap is a modification, and most insurers will ask about it. Many will cover it for a small extra premium or none at all. Not declaring it can invalidate your cover.

Can you put it back to standard?

Yes. We keep your original file, and putting it back is free if you sell the vehicle or need it for a dealer visit.

Will it affect my warranty?

A manufacturer can refuse a claim on parts related to the change. Some ECUs record that they have been reprogrammed even after the original file goes back. If your vehicle is under warranty, we'll talk it through before you decide.

Do you need a workshop?

No. We're fully mobile. All we need is somewhere safe to park, such as your drive, a car park or the yard at work. We bring a battery support unit so the voltage stays steady while we write the file.

Is DPF removal legal?

Not on a road vehicle. Removing or disabling a DPF, EGR or AdBlue system on a UK road vehicle is illegal and fails the MOT. We diagnose and repair these systems, and supply deletes for off-road, motorsport and export vehicles only.

Will it pass the MOT?

A properly calibrated remap keeps smoke levels in check, and we don't touch emissions equipment on road vehicles, so it should pass just as it did before.
